

**Institutions**

- North shifted from believing institutions naturally evolve toward efficiency to recognizing inefficient institutions can persist for centuries. What examples from history best support this later view?
- How might we reconcile the strong correlation between rule of law and GDP with the possibility that causality runs in both directions?
- The chapter summary notes "there is no process analogous to the competitive market process to 'select' the most efficient institutions." If not through market-like competition, how do institutional changes actually occur?
- Comparing the English common law tradition with Roman law systems, what specific mechanisms might explain why one legal system potentially leads to better economic outcomes?
- What explains why some European regions embraced institutional reforms following the French Revolution and Napoleon's invasions, while others resisted similar changes?
- In what ways might the distinction between inclusive and extractive institutions (Acemoglu & Robinson) help explain the divergent paths of post-colonial development seen in different regions?
- Considering the Peruvian Mita example, how do extractive colonial institutions create such persistent economic effects centuries after they officially end?
- How might the different fiscal capacity and interest rates of city-states versus territorial monarchies in medieval Europe help explain divergent economic trajectories?
- North and South Korea represent a compelling natural experiment in institutions. What other historical "natural experiments" might provide insight into the causal impact of institutions on development?
- Greif emphasizes the role of cultural beliefs in constituting institutions. How might this cultural dimension help explain why merely transplanting formal institutions from one society to another often fails?

## Culture

1. Weber proposed the "Protestant Work Ethic" as explaining economic divergence, while Becker and Woessmann suggest it was actually Protestant literacy. How might we distinguish between cultural values versus human capital explanations for the Protestant-Catholic economic gap?
2. The chapter highlights how cultural traits like trust correlate with GDP per capita. Is trust primarily a cause or consequence of economic development, and what evidence could help resolve this question?
3. How do we explain the apparent contradiction that Islamic civilization was economically advanced during its "Golden Age" but many Muslim-majority countries show negative correlation with GDP today? Is this cultural or institutional?
4. The slides note that economists "generally do NOT like using 'culture' as an explanatory variable." Why is this the case, and what methodological challenges exist in studying culture's role in economic development?
5. McCloskey argues a "bourgeois revolution" in cultural values facilitated economic growth, while Rubin claims the Reformation undermined rulers' ability to gain legitimacy through the Church. How might these cultural and institutional explanations complement each other?
6. The persistence of gender norms from plow agriculture suggests cultural values can outlive their original economic purpose. What other examples of "cultural lag" might impede economic development today?
7. Comparing the Habsburg-Ottoman border study with the Italian kinship patterns research, what mechanisms appear to explain the persistence of cultural traits over centuries, even after their original causes disappear?
8. The Chinese Emperor Qianlong publicly dismissed Western innovations while privately seeking to replicate them. What does this suggest about the relationship between stated cultural values and actual economic behaviors?
9. The cultural regions of the United States correspond closely with historical migration patterns and even genetic clustering. How might this persistent regional cultural variation explain economic differences within a single nation?
10. Timur Kuran argues that Islamic economic divergence reflects institutional features of Islamic law rather than cultural values per se. To what extent can we meaningfully separate institutions from culture when analyzing economic development?
