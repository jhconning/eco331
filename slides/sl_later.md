1640–1660, the West Indies attracted over two-thirds of the total number of English emigrants to the Americas. By 1650, there were 44,000 settlers in the West Indies, as compared to 12,000 on the Chesapeake and 23,000 in New England. Most English arrivals were indentured. After five years of labour, they were given "freedom dues" of about £10, usually in goods. Before the mid-1630s, they also received 5 to 10 acres (2 to 4 hectares) of land, but after that time the island filled and there was no more free land. During the Cromwellian era (1650s) this included a large number of prisoners-of-war, vagrants and people who were illicitly kidnapped, who were forcibly transported to the island and sold as servants. 

Cultivation of tobacco, cotton, ginger and indigo was thus handled primarily by European indentured labour until the start of the sugar cane industry in the 1640s and the growing reliance on and importation of African slaves.

The introduction of sugar cane from Dutch Brazil in 1640 completely transformed society, the economy and the physical landscape. Barbados eventually had one of the world's biggest sugar industries.[44]

## A rise in $\frac{T}{L} \rightarrow \frac{w}{r} \uparrow$

- Capital flows to where capital (and land) rents are higher, or potential for capital/land appreciation.

## Allen's "Standard Development Strategy" 

Helped much of Western Europe catch up to Britain.

1. Create large national market
	1. abolish internal tariffs
	2. improve transport
2. Erect an external tariff to protect against British competition
3. Create Banks to provide capital and stabilize the currency
4. Establish Mass education to promote tech adoption and innovation.
