# Slide Audit: sl_4_Malthus.md

## 1. Summary of Current Slide Deck Content & Themes
The slide deck `sl_4_Malthus.md` provides a comprehensive, rigorous overview of pre-industrial demography and the Malthusian Trap. It begins by establishing the stark realities of early life expectancy before diving deep into the formal Malthusian Model (birth/death rates, carrying capacity, technological shocks). It then uses two major historical case studies to illustrate these dynamics: the Irish Potato Famine (as an example of a Malthusian trap and institutional failure) and the Black Death (using Voigtländer & Voth’s "Three Horsemen" argument to show how a mortality shock might shift an economy to a higher steady state). Finally, the deck transitions into Oded Galor's Unified Growth Theory ("The Storm Beneath the Surface" and "Quantys vs. Qualys") before briefly introducing the Solow Growth Model as the post-Malthusian framework for capital accumulation.

## 2. Critiques and Suggested Modifications based on Recent Readings

### A. The Potato Disconnect (Nunn & Qian)
*   **Current State:** The slides dedicate a massive section to the Irish Potato Famine (Slides 256-300) as a textbook example of a Malthusian collapse, monoculture danger, and extractive British institutions. However, the assigned student reading (Nunn & Qian) argues the exact opposite macroeconomic effect: that the potato *positively* fueled urbanization and helped the Old World *escape* Malthusian limits.
*   **Missing Concepts:** The slides currently set up the student presenters to contradict the lecture. The positive, urbanization-driving effects of the Columbian Exchange (the "Biological Channel") are completely omitted.
*   **Suggested Modification:** Before the Famine slides, explicitly teach the *positive* Malthusian shock of the potato (how caloric density allowed for higher populations and urbanization across Europe). Then, pivot to the Irish Famine as a specific cautionary tale of what happens when that demographic boom is paired with monoculture and extractive institutions.

### B. The European Marriage Pattern (Koyama & Rubin 5)
*   **Current State:** Slide 241 shows a chart titled "European Marriage and Fertility" but contains no explanatory text.
*   **Missing Concepts:** Koyama & Rubin emphasize the "European Marriage Pattern" (EMP)—characterized by late marriage and high permanent celibacy—as a crucial "preventative check" that created a low-pressure demographic regime in Northwestern Europe. This is a vital mechanism for understanding how Europe avoided the harshest Malthusian outcomes.
*   **Suggested Modification:** Add text to Slide 241 explicitly defining the EMP. Explain how delaying marriage served as a Malthusian preventative check and potentially fostered early human capital investment (as women entered the labor force earlier).

### C. Evolutionary Selection (Galor OG 3)
*   **Current State:** The "Quantys vs Qualys" section (Slides 409-432) beautifully illustrates the trade-off between the quantity and quality of children.
*   **Missing Concepts:** Galor explicitly frames this shift as an *evolutionary selection* process (both cultural and biological). The harsh Malthusian environment actively selected for lineages that invested in human capital, gradually changing the composition of the population.
*   **Suggested Modification:** Explicitly use the term "Evolutionary Selection" in the "Quantys vs Qualys" slides to reinforce Galor's argument that the Malthusian trap was actually an incubation period that systematically altered human preferences.
