# AI-Era Course Activities for ECO 331

Ideas for integrating AI tools into Economic History course while encouraging critical thinking, class participation, and maintaining easy grading.

## Core Principle

Students are encouraged to use AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) as learning complements, with transparency about how they're used. Activities should promote engagement while being grading-efficient.

## Technology Integration

**TopHat (tophat.com):** Free for Hunter students, provides:
- Attendance tracking (integrated with participation grades)
- Live in-class polling and voting
- Various question types (MC, numeric, click-on-target, word clouds)
- Student response analytics

**Canvas LMS:** Primary platform for assignments, discussion boards, and grading

---

## Weekly AI Debate Briefs

**Structure:** Assign rotating teams each week to prepare "debate briefs" on a central question using AI assistance.

**Example topics:**
- "Was the Industrial Revolution primarily driven by institutions or geography?"
- "Did colonialism cause or prevent development in colonized regions?"
- "Were enclosures primarily beneficial or harmful?"

**Student deliverable (due before class):**
- A 2-page brief with:
  - 3 strongest arguments FOR (developed with AI help)
  - 3 strongest arguments AGAINST (developed with AI help)
  - 2 key pieces of historical evidence for each side
  - Their "AI conversation log" link or summary (transparency requirement)

**Grading rubric (simple):**
- Completeness (all elements present): 40%
- Evidence quality: 30%
- AI transparency: 20%
- Originality of synthesis: 10%

**In-class:** 10-minute structured debate between teams

**TopHat integration:**
- Live polling: Students vote on most persuasive argument (with analytics showing voting patterns)
- Follow-up poll: "Which piece of evidence was most convincing?" (multiple choice)
- Attendance credit automatically recorded

## AI Fact-Check Challenge

**Activity:** Students deliberately test AI knowledge on specific historical claims from readings.

**Assignment:** Each student submits (via Canvas):
1. One historical claim from the week's reading (e.g., "Diamond argues geography determined which societies domesticated animals")
2. What AI said when asked about this claim
3. A 3-sentence evaluation: Did AI get it right? What did it miss? What did it hallucinate?

**Grading:** Pass/fail or 3-point scale (Incomplete/Adequate/Excellent)

**Value:** Teaches critical evaluation of AI while reinforcing reading comprehension

## Pre-Class "Argument Mining" (Modified)

**Enhancement to current pre-class questions:** Instead of just posting questions and comments, students must:

1. **Use AI to explore one question** from the reading
2. **Post:**
   - Original question
   - Most interesting insight from AI conversation
   - One follow-up question AI couldn't answer well (brings to class)

**Grading:** Completion-based (posted on time = full credit). Grade on participation, not depth.

**In-class payoff:** Start class with "Here are the top 5 questions AI struggled with from your submissions" → immediate engagement

**TopHat integration:**
- Display word cloud of most common topics students struggled with
- Quick poll: "Which question should we tackle first?" (students vote)
- Discussion board responses visible during class discussion

## Counterfactual Scenario Analysis (Monthly)

**Assignment:** Students use AI to explore historical counterfactuals, then present findings.

**Examples:**
- "What if China had colonized the Americas instead of Europe?"
- "What if the Black Death had killed 80% instead of 30-50% of Europe?"
- "What if Britain had abolished slavery in 1700 instead of 1833?"

**Deliverable:**
- 1-page scenario summary (co-developed with AI)
- 1 chart/diagram (created by student based on AI suggestions)
- 3-minute in-class presentation

**Grading criteria:**
- Historical plausibility: 40%
- Economic reasoning: 40%
- Presentation clarity: 20%

**Rotation:** 3-4 students present per class throughout semester

## Historiography Through AI

**Activity:** Students use AI to understand different schools of thought.

**Prompt template you provide:**
> "Explain [topic] from the perspective of: (a) A Marxist historian, (b) A new institutional economist, (c) A geographic determinist. What would each emphasize? Where would they disagree?"

**Assignment:** Students submit:
- AI's response
- Their 1-paragraph synthesis identifying the key theoretical divide
- Which perspective they find most convincing and why (1 paragraph)

**Grading:** 5-point scale, completion-based with quality bonus

**Benefit:** Helps students see multiple analytical frameworks, builds critical thinking

## Live "AI Panel Debate" (In-Class)

**Setup:** Divide class into 4 groups, each assigned a perspective (Marxist, Institutionalist, Geographic Determinist, Cultural Evolutionist)

**Pre-class (10 mins homework):** Each student uses AI to understand their assigned perspective on the day's topic

**In-class (20 mins):**
- Groups huddle (5 mins) to compile best arguments
- "Panel debate" format: Each group gets 2 mins opening, then open discussion
- You moderate, students can consult AI in real-time for evidence

**Assessment:** Participation-based (were they engaged? Did they contribute?)

**Twist:** Final 5 mins, students vote for most persuasive argument *from a perspective other than their assigned one*

**TopHat integration:**
- Real-time polling during debate: "Is this argument convincing?" (thumbs up/down)
- Heat map showing which arguments resonated most
- Final vote on best argument (excludes votes for own team)
- Attendance + participation credit auto-recorded

## Primary Source Analysis with AI Assistance

**Assignment:** Provide a primary source excerpt (e.g., Adam Smith, Marx, colonial documents)

**Task:**
1. Read the source independently
2. Ask AI to explain the historical context
3. Ask AI to identify the author's main argument
4. Post: "What did AI get right? What did it miss? What would you add?"

**Grading:** Completion + quality of critique (3-point scale)

**Extension:** Best critiques become discussion starters in class

## Economic Model Explainer Videos (Creative Option)

**Assignment:** Students use AI to understand an economic model (Malthusian trap, unified growth theory, institutional development)

**Deliverable:** 3-minute explainer video where they:
- Explain the model in their own words
- Use one AI-generated visualization/diagram
- Apply it to a specific historical case

**Grading rubric:**
- Accuracy: 40%
- Clarity: 40%
- Application: 20%

**Bonus:** Best videos become study resources for the class

## Weekly Evidence Hunt (Minimal Grading)

**Activity:** Each week, 2-3 students are "evidence curators"

**Their job:** Find 3 pieces of evidence (data, charts, historical examples) that support or challenge a key claim from the readings—using AI to help locate sources

**Deliverable:** Post to Canvas discussion board with:
- Evidence
- Source
- Brief explanation (2 sentences)

**Grading:** Completion-based

**In-class:** Reference the best evidence during lecture, students who posted get recognition

**TopHat integration:**
- Display evidence on slides during discussion
- Quick poll: "Does this evidence support or challenge the claim?" (scale 1-5)
- Recognition leaderboard for best curators

## TopHat-Specific Activities

### 1. "Claim Testing" - Live Class Activity

**Setup:** Display a historical claim on TopHat (e.g., "The Industrial Revolution started in Britain because of coal")

**Activity flow (10 minutes):**
1. **Initial poll:** "Do you agree?" (Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree, 1-5 scale)
2. **AI consultation (3 mins):** Students use phones to ask AI about evidence for/against
3. **Discussion:** Share what AI found (you moderate)
4. **Final poll:** Same question - see if opinions shifted
5. **Analytics:** Display before/after comparison, discuss why views changed

**Grading:** Participation credit (answered both polls)

**Value:** Shows how evidence investigation changes beliefs; reveals common misconceptions

### 2. Historical Data Interpretation

**Setup:** Display a chart/graph (e.g., population growth, GDP per capita, slave trade volumes)

**TopHat question types:**
- **Click-on-target:** "Click on the graph where you see the Industrial Revolution begin"
- **Numeric response:** "What % did population grow between 1750-1850?"
- **Short answer:** "What's one explanation for this trend?"

**AI integration:** Students can consult AI to interpret data, then submit answers

**Follow-up:** Word cloud of explanations, discuss most common interpretations

### 3. "Predict the Historian" Game

**Setup:** Present a historical question (e.g., "Why did the Black Death end feudalism?")

**Activity:**
1. **Poll:** Students predict: "What would a Marxist historian emphasize?" (multiple choice)
2. **AI check:** Students ask AI for Marxist perspective
3. **Reveal:** Show correct answer + brief explanation
4. **Repeat** for Institutionalist, Geographic Determinist perspectives

**TopHat features:**
- Leaderboard showing who predicted correctly
- Time-limited responses (builds excitement)
- Instant feedback with explanations

**Value:** Gamifies learning different analytical frameworks; highly engaging

### 4. Counterfactual Polling

**Before presentations:** Students present counterfactual scenarios (see "Counterfactual Scenario Analysis")

**TopHat enhancement:**
- **Before presentation:** "How plausible is this scenario?" (poll)
- **After presentation:** Same poll (see if presenter convinced class)
- **Discussion question:** "What was most/least convincing?" (open-ended)
- Analytics show persuasion effectiveness

### 5. Debate Scoring in Real-Time

**During debates:** Students rate arguments as they're presented

**TopHat features:**
- Live scoring: Rate each argument 1-5 on "Evidence Quality" and "Logic"
- Heat map showing which arguments resonated most
- Final tally determines winning team
- Export data for grading participation

### 6. Pre-Class Concept Check

**Use TopHat's homework feature:** Due before class

**Question types:**
- MC: "According to Diamond, which of these was NOT a reason for Eurasian advantages?"
- Numeric: "In what year did the British abolish slavery in their colonies?"
- Short answer: "Name one criticism of geographic determinism from the readings"

**AI policy:** Students CAN use AI but must understand answers (you'll discuss in class)

**In-class:** Display results, focus discussion on questions most students missed

### 7. Exit Tickets with AI Reflection

**End of class (last 5 minutes):**

**TopHat questions:**
1. "What's the most important thing you learned today?" (short answer)
2. "What question do you still have?" (short answer)
3. "Did you use AI to help understand today's topic?" (Yes/No)
4. If yes: "Was it helpful?" (Very/Somewhat/Not really)

**Value:**
- Immediate feedback on comprehension
- Track AI usage patterns
- Identify topics needing clarification
- Analytics show trends over semester

## Implementation Strategy

### Start Small - Spring 2026

**Easy wins (modify existing assignments):**
- AI Fact-Check Challenge (Canvas submission)
- Argument Mining (enhancement to current pre-class questions)
- Exit Tickets (TopHat - every class)
- Pre-Class Concept Check (TopHat - weekly)

**Higher engagement:**
- Weekly Debate Briefs (rotating teams, TopHat voting)
- Live AI Panel Debate (2-3 times per semester with TopHat polling)
- "Claim Testing" live activity (TopHat - bi-weekly)
- "Predict the Historian" game (TopHat - monthly)

**Deeper learning:**
- Counterfactual Analysis (once per month, with TopHat polling)
- Historiography Through AI (3-4 times per semester)

### Grading Efficiency Tips

- Use completion-based grading for 70% of assignments
- Reserve detailed grading for 2-3 major submissions
- Use Canvas rubrics for quick grading
- Have students peer-review selected assignments
- Create templates for common feedback

### AI Transparency Requirement

**Add to syllabus:**
> "When using AI tools, students must include either: (1) a link to the conversation, (2) a screenshot, or (3) a brief summary of how AI was used. This is about transparency, not restriction."

- **Debate:** "Do institutions cause growth or does growth create good institutions?"

- **Panel debate:** Commercialization model vs. Marxist explanations

- **Debate:** "Why Britain first?"

- **Debate:** "Did slavery/colonialism enrich Europe or delay development?"
- **Evidence hunt:** Quantitative studies on long-term effects
- **Primary source:** Colonial documents, slave trade records

## Sample TopHat + AI Activity Flow

**Example: Topic on Institutions and Economic Growth**

### Before Class (Canvas + AI)
- **Reading:** North (1990), Acemoglu & Robinson excerpts
- **Assignment:** Argument Mining (due night before)
  - Students ask AI: "What's the institutional explanation for economic growth?"
  - Post: AI's answer + one follow-up question AI struggled with

### Class Session 1: Introduction (TopHat + Discussion)

**Opening (5 mins):**
- TopHat attendance check (automatic)
- Word cloud: "What institution did readings emphasize most?"

**Lecture/Discussion (30 mins):**
- Your presentation on institutional theory
- Display student questions AI couldn't answer
- TopHat poll: "Do institutions cause growth or vice versa?" (pre-instruction baseline)

**"Claim Testing" Activity (15 mins):**
- Display: "Strong property rights always lead to economic growth"
- Initial poll (agree/disagree scale)
- Students consult AI for evidence (3 mins)
- Discussion of findings
- Final poll (see if opinions shifted with evidence)

**"Predict the Historian" Game (10 mins):**
- Q: "What would Acemoglu emphasize about colonialism's effects?"
- Students answer via TopHat (timed)
- Reveal answer + brief explanation
- Leaderboard shows top scorers

**Exit Ticket (3 mins):**
- "Most important thing learned today?"
- "Remaining question?"
- "Used AI today?" (track usage)

### Between Classes (Canvas)
- AI Fact-Check assignment: Test specific institutional claims from readings
- Evidence Hunt team posts data on institutional quality and growth

### Class Session 2: Debate (TopHat + Presentations)

**Debate Prep (Canvas, due before class):**
- Two teams prepared debate briefs on: "Did institutions or geography matter more for the Industrial Revolution?"

**Debate Structure (40 mins):**
- Team presentations (10 mins each)
- TopHat live polling during arguments:
  - "Is this evidence convincing?" (thumbs up/down)
  - Analytics show which arguments resonate
- Cross-examination
- Final vote (excluding own team)
- Heat map shows most persuasive arguments

**Synthesis (15 mins):**
- Your commentary on debate
- TopHat poll: "Has your view changed from last class?"
- Connect to upcoming readings

**Exit Ticket (3 mins)**

### After Class
- TopHat analytics review:
  - Which concepts need clarification?
  - How many used AI effectively?
  - Attendance patterns
- Adjust next class based on data

## Benefits of TopHat + AI Integration

### For Students:
- **Immediate engagement:** Active participation every class
- **Safe exploration:** Can test ideas via AI before speaking up
- **Anonymous contribution:** Polling allows participation without calling on individuals
- **Visible learning:** See how evidence changes opinions in real-time
- **Accountability:** Attendance tracked, participation rewarded

### For Instructor:
- **Automatic attendance:** No manual tracking needed
- **Real-time assessment:** See comprehension gaps immediately
- **Data-driven adjustments:** Analytics show what's working
- **Easy grading:** Participation auto-tracked
- **Engagement metrics:** Track which activities work best

### For Learning Outcomes:
- **Critical thinking:** Testing AI responses against evidence
- **Multiple perspectives:** Quick exposure to different frameworks
- **Evidence evaluation:** Constant practice assessing claims
- **Metacognition:** Exit tickets promote reflection
- **Peer learning:** See classmates' reasoning via polls/word clouds

## TopHat Best Practices

### Setup:
- Create question folders by topic (reuse/refine each semester)
- Set default points for participation questions (low stakes)
- Enable anonymous responses where appropriate (encourages honesty)
- Connect to Canvas gradebook (automatic sync)

### During Class:
- Keep polls brief (30 seconds max response time)
- Always show results after polling (creates "aha" moments)
- Mix question types (keeps students alert)
- Use leaderboards sparingly (can be motivating or stressful)

### Grading Philosophy:
- **Completion > Correctness** for most activities
- Award points for engagement, not always being right
- Higher points for prepared activities (debate briefs)
- Bonus points for "best evidence" or "most improved" participation

### Integration Tips:
- Pre-load questions before class (smoother flow)
- Have backup plan if tech fails (can happen)
- Test questions with small group first
- Build question library over semester (reuse/refine)
