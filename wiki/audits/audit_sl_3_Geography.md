# Slide Audit: sl_3_Geography.md

## 1. Summary of Current Slide Deck Content & Themes
The slide deck `sl_3_Geography.md` investigates whether physical environments dictate economic destiny. It begins by distinguishing between "fundamental" causes of growth (like geography or institutions) and "proximate" causes (like capital accumulation). Interestingly, a significant portion of the early slides (Slides 83-206) is dedicated to an extensive primer on econometrics—covering linear regression, omitted variable bias, and difference-in-differences (revisiting the Allen et al. Mesopotamia paper). 

The deck then pivots to empirical geography, exploring how landlocked status (Sachs), disease environments (Malaria, Tsetse flies), climate variability (Roman warm periods), and Diamond's continental axis theory shape development. It balances this determinism by showing how human agency—via infrastructure like Roman roads—can overcome geographic barriers. Finally, the deck touches on the Columbian Exchange (specifically the potato) and how agroclimates might influence cultural norms like future-orientation and gender roles.

## 2. Critiques and Suggested Modifications based on Recent Readings

### A. Galor (OG 10) & Koyama/Rubin (KR 2): The Limits of Determinism
*   **Current State:** The deck provides excellent empirical examples of geography hindering development (malaria, tsetse). 
*   **Missing Concepts:** Both KR and Galor emphasize the *limits* of geographic determinism. KR specifically argues that geography cannot explain the *timing* of the Industrial Revolution or the "Reversal of Fortune" (why rich colonized areas became poor). Galor argues that geography casts a long "shadow" by influencing the *incentives* for human capital and institutional formation, but it is not absolute fate. 
*   **Suggested Modification:** The slides ask "Is geography fate?" (Slide 72) but never decisively answer it using the readings. Add a synthesis slide explicitly rejecting strict environmental determinism, using KR's "Reversal of Fortune" or Galor's "Shadow vs. Destiny" concepts as the counter-evidence.

### B. Nunn & Qian: The Potato's Contribution
*   **Current State:** The slides have a section titled "The Columbian Exchange: The Potato" (Slides 343-369), but it consists entirely of commented-out images or title cards without explaining the underlying economic mechanism. Nunn & Qian are listed as the student focus paper at the very end.
*   **Missing Concepts:** The core mechanism of the paper—that the potato provided a massive, high-caloric shock to the Old World that eased Malthusian constraints and directly fueled urbanization and population growth—is entirely missing from the visual presentation.
*   **Suggested Modification:** Un-comment the potato slides and add bullet points detailing the Malthusian escape mechanism identified by Nunn & Qian.

### C. The Econometrics Primer Disconnect
*   **Current State:** The deck spends roughly 30 slides teaching basic linear regression and omitted variable bias, culminating in a repeat of the Allen et al. (Mesopotamia) Diff-in-Diff study from the previous module.
*   **Critique:** While methodological rigor is important, it severely derails the pacing of a lecture fundamentally about Geography. The Mesopotamia example, while geographically adjacent (rivers), is fundamentally about state formation and was already covered in `sl_2_EarlyStates`.

### Suggested slide deletion or reorganization
* **Relocate Econometrics:** Consider moving the massive econometrics primer (Slides 83-206) into a standalone "Methods Appendix" or a separate introductory lecture. If kept, severely condense it. 
* **Remove Duplicate Mesopotamia Slides:** Delete the Allen et al. Diff-in-Diff slides (175-190) as they are a direct repeat of the content from `sl_2_EarlyStates.md`.

### Suggested slide additions
* **Add a "Limits of Geography" Slide:** Introduce the concept of the "Reversal of Fortune" (KR 2) to demonstrate that geography alone cannot explain long-run development trajectories or the timing of the Industrial Revolution.
* **Add Nunn & Qian Mechanisms:** Add 1-2 slides explicitly detailing the "Biological Channel" of the Columbian Exchange—specifically how the potato's caloric efficiency allowed Europe to escape the Malthusian trap and urbanize.
