# Slide Audit: sl_9_IR.md

## 1. Summary of Current Slide Deck Content & Themes
The slide deck `sl_9_IR.md` provides a comprehensive overview of the British Industrial Revolution, focusing on the period from 1700 to 1870. It begins by outlining the preconditions for growth, including commercialization, market integration, and significant transport improvements (canals and turnpikes). The deck then visualizes several demographic trends through charts, including rising literacy, child labor, and fertility rates.

The core of the presentation explores the technological innovations of the era, specifically in textiles (e.g., the spinning jenny, water frame, spinning mule) and steam power (Newcomen and Watt engines). In the latter half, the slides transition into an analytical comparison of competing theories for *why* Britain industrialized first, drawing from Koyama and Rubin's Chapter 8. The deck contrasts Mokyr's "Culture of Growth" and Industrial Enlightenment with Robert Allen's "High-Wage Economy." It concludes with a deep dive into Allen's model of directed technical change, utilizing interactive Geogebra models to explain technology adoption based on capital vs. labor factor prices, and the slow diffusion of technologies like power looms.

## 2. Critiques and Suggested Modifications based on Recent Readings

### A. Oded Galor: "Full Steam" (The Demographic Transition)
*   **Current State:** The slides feature several charts from Galor showcasing total fertility rates (TFR), human capital, and literacy, but these visuals lack accompanying explanatory text detailing Galor's core mechanisms.
*   **Missing Concepts:** The assigned reading emphasizes the "Quantity vs. Quality" trade-off. Galor argues that the acceleration of technological progress during the Industrial Revolution increased the economic return on education. Consequently, parents shifted their resources toward educating a smaller number of children, facilitating a demographic transition that definitively broke the Malthusian trap.
*   **Suggested Modification:** Add explicit text slides alongside or immediately following the Galor charts. These slides should define the "Quantity vs. Quality" trade-off and explicitly state that this shift to human capital investment is what ultimately allowed output per capita to escape population growth constraints.

### B. Joel Mokyr: "The Years of Miracles" (Macro vs. Microinventions)
*   **Current State:** The deck successfully mentions Mokyr’s "Culture of Growth," the Republic of Letters, and the Royal Society. However, it does not incorporate the specific analytical framework from *The Lever of Riches*.
*   **Missing Concepts:** The assigned chapter emphasizes the distinction between "macroinventions" (radical, unprecedented leaps that break bottlenecks, like the Newcomen engine) and "microinventions" (incremental, continuous improvements by skilled mechanics, like Watt's separate condenser). The slides currently list inventions sequentially without this categorization.
*   **Suggested Modification:** Introduce a slide that defines and distinguishes between macroinventions and microinventions. Apply this framework to the existing slides on steam power, framing the Newcomen engine as a macroinvention and Watt's improvements as a blend of macro and microinventions resulting from collaborative engineering.

### C. Koyama & Rubin: Synthesis of Competing Theories
*   **Current State:** The slides present Mokyr and Allen as competing theories but dedicate the vast majority of analytical focus (and interactive models) to Allen's model of factor prices (wages vs. energy).
*   **Missing Concepts:** Koyama & Rubin present these theories as highly complementary rather than mutually exclusive. Innovation required both the *incentive* provided by high wages (Allen) and the *capability* provided by an enlightened culture and skilled human capital base (Mokyr). The slides currently leave students with a skewed impression toward pure economic incentives.
*   **Suggested Modification:** Add a capstone synthesis slide after the Allen Geogebra models. Ask students to consider whether Allen's incentives would have worked without Mokyr's capabilities (and vice versa), prompting a discussion on how these two forces operated together to spark the Industrial Revolution.

## 3. Conclusion and Action Items

### Suggested slide deletion or reorganization
*   Reorganize the steam engine and textile innovation slides to explicitly group them using Mokyr's "macroinvention" and "microinvention" terminology.
*   Condense some of the Geogebra interactive slides for Allen's model to make room for a more balanced conclusion that brings Mokyr's cultural/human capital theories back into focus.

### Suggested slide additions
*   Add 1-2 text slides accompanying the demographic charts to explicitly define Galor's "Quantity vs. Quality" trade-off and its role in breaking the Malthusian trap.
*   Add a slide introducing the macroinvention vs. microinvention framework from *The Lever of Riches*.
*   Add a final Synthesis/Discussion slide prompting students to reconcile Allen’s high-wage incentives with Mokyr’s culture of innovation.
