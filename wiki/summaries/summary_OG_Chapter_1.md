# The Journey of Humanity: Chapter 1 - First Steps

> **Source:** OG 1 "First Steps," 13-25. (use Amazon Read Sample if you don't have book yet)


## Overview
In the opening chapter of *The Journey of Humanity*, Oded Galor frames the narrative of human history through the lens of the "Mystery of Growth." He presents the stark contrast between the vast majority of human existence, characterized by stagnation and near-universal poverty, and the extraordinary economic takeoff of the last two centuries. Galor establishes the central paradox of human history: while technological progress is the engine of prosperity, it paradoxically kept humanity trapped in poverty for millennia until the relatively recent transition to sustained economic growth.

## Core Arguments & Mechanisms

*   **The Malthusian Trap:** For most of human history, technological advancement did not lead to an increase in living standards. Instead, any gains in productivity were quickly consumed by population growth. This mechanism—the Malthusian trap—ensured that per capita income remained stagnant, tethered to the "subsistence level."
*   **The Technological-Demographic Feedback Loop:** Galor argues that during the long epoch of stagnation, there was a positive feedback loop between technology and population size. Greater human density allowed for more brains, more ideas, and more innovation. However, because technological gains were absorbed by population increases, humanity remained at a subsistence equilibrium rather than experiencing cumulative growth in wealth.
*   **The Great Divergence:** The transition out of this epoch did not occur simultaneously across the globe. Galor introduces the concept of the "Great Divergence," where the rapid acceleration of growth in Western nations left other regions behind, creating vast disparities in wealth that are the focal point of modern economic investigation.
*   **The Mystery of Growth:** The core question posed is not just why some countries are rich and others poor today, but why humanity spent 300,000 years in the "Malthusian Epoch" before suddenly breaking free into a period of unprecedented expansion in education, health, and per capita income.

## Connections to Course Themes

*   **The Malthusian Epoch:** This chapter serves as the foundational definition of the Malthusian period. Students should understand this not merely as a historical curiosity, but as an economic regime where the elasticity of population with respect to income was high, preventing permanent improvements in the standard of living.
*   **Economic History vs. Growth Theory:** Galor bridges the gap between traditional neoclassical growth models and deep-time economic history. He argues that modern growth theory is insufficient if it ignores the thousands of years of stagnation that preceded the Industrial Revolution.
*   **Institutions, Culture, and Geography:** While Chapter 1 focuses on the broad arc of human history, it lays the groundwork for later course inquiries into why specific geographies and cultural traits (such as the shift in preference toward child quality over child quantity) acted as the catalysts that finally broke the Malthusian mechanism.
*   **The Demographic Transition:** Galor provides the necessary preface to the Demographic Transition. By identifying that humanity was "trapped" by population pressure, he sets the stage for our upcoming discussion on how a change in the economic value of children allowed nations to break the link between economic growth and population explosion.
