### What Are Institutions?
- **Douglas North**: "Rules of the game" — humanly devised constraints shaping human interaction
- Formal (laws, constitutions) and informal (customs, norms)
- Reduce uncertainty; enable commitment, trust, investment, and trade

### Brief History of Political Economy Ideas
| School | Core View |
|---|---|
| Autocracy/Mercantilism | Zero-sum; monopolies, rent extraction, state intervention |
| Classical Liberals (Locke, Smith, Hayek) | Natural rights, limited government, rule of law, free markets |
| Marx | Institutions reflect class interests; shaped by class conflict |
| Neo-classical | Institutions assumed away; markets → efficiency |
| Keynesian/Development | Market failures real; state needed |
| Neo-institutionalists (Coase, North) | Institutions are the *deep cause* of growth |

### Neo-classical Growth & Its Limits
- Differences in income → differences in technology and K/L ratios
- Solow residual: only ~13% of growth explained by factor accumulation
- New Growth Theory: knowledge spillovers, increasing returns, but institutions still assumed

### Neo-Institutional Economics
- **Coase**: Firms exist to minimize *transactions costs*
- **North**: Capital and innovation are *proximate* causes; institutions are *deeper* causes
- Early North: institutions evolve toward efficiency
- Later North: elites shape institutions to their benefit → inefficient institutions persist

### Factor Endowments and Paths of Development (Sokoloff & Engerman)
- Caribbean/Brazil (sugar+slaves) → extreme inequality → extractive institutions
- Spanish America (mines + native labor) → concentrated wealth → restricted access
- North America (no plantation agriculture) → broad land distribution → inclusive institutions
- Initial inequality → persistent inequality via policies on voting, education, credit, land

### Key Natural Experiments
- **Korea**: Same geography/culture, divergent institutions → 20x income gap
- **AJR (2001)**: Settler mortality → settlement patterns → colonial institutions → income today
- **Napoleonic Codes**: French occupation → better-defined property rights → higher long-run growth in Germany
- **Dissolution of Monasteries (1535)**: Land redistribution → rise of gentry → agricultural and industrial development

### Glorious Revolution (1688)
- Parliament constrained the Crown → credible commitment → lower borrowing costs, secure property rights
- *Critique*: may have strengthened state power for rising commercial class rather than rights broadly

### Inclusive vs. Extractive Institutions (Acemoglu & Robinson)
- **Inclusive**: broad distribution of power, free entry, secure PRs → growth
- **Extractive**: power concentrated, rent extraction → stagnation
- Representative institutions (city-states, parliaments) → lower interest rates, more investment

### The Peruvian Mita
- Spanish forced labor tax on conquered Inca → persistent underdevelopment in affected regions
