Google Scholar is a way to search academic papers and books. [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org/) is a powerful free open-source reference manager that will help you keep track of the references you find. I would recommend that you use them both together, in which case you'll want to install and configure Zotero as described at the end of this page, and then read the Google Scholar description. If you just want to use Google Scholar, read on.

## Google Scholar

[Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/) is one of several valuable resources for performing academic research searches.

**Setup:** If you go to the page and click on the three lines next to the "Google Scholar" you'll see a "Settings" option, and from there "Library Links" option. If you then type in "Hunter College" you will set things up for the search results to link directly to Hunter Library resources, for example many articles will become available in PDF format.

**Search Example:** Say you were interested in Ghana as a country, and in particular in the period of the growth of exports. You go to Google Scholar and you search for "Ghana history cocoa".

You obtain the following. The results are sorted by relevance (e.g. how close the title of the article is to your search) but higher citation count papers also rise higher in the search results. You will notice here that the second result, a book by P. Hill, has a "Cited by 1276". That's a very high citation count which suggests this is a very influential study.

You will see some results are articles and full-text PDF is available via the Hunter library.

You could start downloading and reading some of these resources, but you could also use other online tools such as ChatGPT to help. [ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com/) will in general be able to give good summaries of highly cited books. Here is what we get.

That gives us a few clues for doing further searches (you'll get much more detail if you don't specify you want the answer in bullet form).

Google Scholar allows you to filter searches in sophisticated ways. For example, suppose we want to search for material that cites the P. Hill book and also mentions "migrant cocoa farmers". We can do this by clicking on the "Cited by 1276" below the P. Hill reference. This takes us to a screen that lets us search just that. In the example I've searched for "Ghana cocoa property rights" after having clicked on the "search within citing articles".

![Google Scholar "cited by" search results filtered to articles citing P. Hill, with a secondary search for "Ghana cocoa property rights" showing recent empirical studies](https://canvas.instructure.com/courses/14011875/files/331001715/preview?verifier=2mgl12tXJFa0cHAVPjmGhLmnjmYb5cg1N1kvk3oq)

Note how you can also narrow the date range. We could, for example, ask for studies written after 2010, etc. Searches like this help us start to narrow things down. For example, the results above suggest an interesting historical and empirical literature on the evolution of property rights in Ghana, which I can continue to explore.

How does Zotero fit in? If you've installed Zotero with its browser extension, you can quickly pull the bibliography items you want into the Zotero database in a way that will allow you to cite things later, create a bibliography, etc.

## Zotero

  * [Download Zotero](https://www.zotero.org/)
  * **Configure:** Open Zotero on your device, go to the Tools menu and choose "Install Browser Connector" (or go to the [Zotero connectors page](https://www.zotero.org/download/connectors)). This will allow you to install an extension on your browser of choice (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari).



With this in place, any time you are on a Google Scholar page (or Amazon page, or any web page with bibliography information) you can just click on the Zotero extension icon that will appear in your browser to add a selection of the items on the page into your bibliography collection. For example, back to that Ghana example, if I click on the connector I get a popup like this:

![Zotero browser connector popup on a Google Scholar page, showing a list of search results with checkboxes to select items for import into Zotero](https://canvas.instructure.com/courses/14011875/files/331001721/preview?verifier=2D17PQvjjLlOtdTg4XaXZcO49fjR0Ez7bCUHc8k8)

Suppose I want to bring in the article by Bubb on "The evolution of property rights..." — I just place a checkmark next to the item and press OK and it will be entered in my Zotero database. Here is how the entry will then look in my desktop version of Zotero. Notice that Zotero has not only pulled in the bibliographic information but also a PDF of the article itself, which is now stored in the database (you can also set up a Zotero account that will store all info online to sync across your devices).
