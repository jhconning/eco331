### Henri Pirenne
- According to Pirenne, the Roman Empire and its civilization did not significantly decline due to Germanic invasions in the 5th century as traditionally thought. Instead, he argued that Mediterranean economic and cultural unity continued well after the fall of Rome, with Germanic kings largely maintaining Roman institutions, tax systems, and commercial networks. Closing off trade routes between East and West.
- The real break with classical antiquity, according to Pirenne, came with the Islamic conquests of the 7th and 8th centuries. As Muslim forces conquered North Africa, Egypt, and much of the Mediterranean coastline, they disrupted the traditional Mediterranean trade networks that had sustained Europe's economy. This disruption forced Europe to become more self-sufficient and inward-looking, leading to:

	1. The shift of economic power northward away from the Mediterranean
	2. The rise of a more feudal, manorial economic system
	3. The development of a distinctly "European" civilization centered around the Carolingian Empire

A growing 'economy of exchange' led by a class of professional merchangs, replaced by 'an economy of consumption,' the rentier economy of the fuedal aristocracy.

But eventually, according to the hypothesis, cities emerged with autonomy. Cities devotged to trade and dominated by an autonomous burgher (or Bourgeiois) class
